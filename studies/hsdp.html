Shard only over NVLink? HSDP for DeepSeek-V3

DRAFT (unlisted study). Prose below is AI-drafted and awaits a human edit pass — see the disclosure footnote.1

This picks up from the FSDP section of post 03, which prices DeepSeek-V3 on 2,048 H800s with EP64 and no pipeline parallelism. Without PP, memory forces eight microbatches of one sequence each: one sequence's activation stash is 38.9 GiB, and two don't fit. The best variant there keeps the routed experts' weights resident and still needs 11.0 s of InfiniBand per step against 6.8 s of compute. Its gathers are already hierarchical: seven-eighths of every non-expert gather travels over NVLink, and only the last eighth crosses IB.

So: what if we shard only across NVLink? If no shard group spans InfiniBand, do we stop being exposed on it? Hybrid sharding (HSDP) does exactly this, sharding within a group and replicating across groups. The answer comes in three parts. Shrinking the group helps only if it shrinks all the way to one node. One node technically fits in memory. And the step is still exposed: on IB because of the experts, and on an H800, on NVLink too.2

Only the node helps

With a hierarchical gather over a shard group of k nodes, a node already holds 1/k of the parameters and pulls the other (k − 1)/k across IB, split over its eight NICs. So each non-expert move (a gather, or a reduce-scatter of the gradients) puts (1 − 1/k) of an eighth of the BF16 non-expert weights, 4.28 GB, through each GPU's NIC:

Shrinking the group from 256 nodes to 16, or even to 4, buys almost nothing on IB and costs memory: every gather still brings in nearly the whole parameter over IB. The 1 − 1/k factor only matters at two nodes and only vanishes at one. There's no useful middle. A shard group that crosses nodes pays nearly full-FSDP IB, and one inside the node pays none.

At one node, what IB sees is plain data parallelism. There are no per-layer gathers, only a once-per-step gradient reduce-scatter and parameter all-gather across the 256 replicas, 0.26 s. All the sharding has moved inside the node. Combined with the optimizer sharding below, the non-experts end up running ZeRO-1 data parallelism across nodes with FSDP inside each node.

It technically fits

Plain HSDP keeps a whole copy of the non-expert state in every node: 14 bytes per parameter over eight GPUs is 27.9 GiB each, and with the experts and the stash that's 89.4 GiB, against the H800's 79.65. What does fit is sharding only the BF16 weights and FP32 gradient accumulators within the node, and the FP32 master weights and Adam moments (8 of the 14 bytes) over all 2,048 GPUs, as in ZeRO-1. That saves 15.9 GiB, and IB doesn't charge for it: ZeRO-1's reduce-scatter and all-gather are the same bytes as a data-parallel all-reduce.

Each piece fits for its own reason, and none of them would on its own:

  • Non-experts: both kinds of sharding. Plain ZeRO-1 data parallelism would hold the full BF16 weights and FP32 gradients on every GPU, 95.7 GiB of non-expert state alone. Sharding those 6 bytes eight ways inside the node is what gets it to 12.0 GiB.
  • Experts: EP does the work. The routed experts are 97% of the parameters, but each GPU holds only its 1/64 slice: 19.0 GiB of BF16 weights, plus 3.6 GiB of FP32 gradient, master and moments sharded over the slice's 32 replicas. HSDP can't shrink this further: no node-mate holds our slice, so there's nothing in the node to shard it with.
  • Stash: one sequence at a time. The largest item, and the reason microbatching is forced.

"Technically" is about the remaining 6.1 GiB. The total leaves out the buffer holding the layer currently gathered, communication buffers and allocator fragmentation, and those could plausibly use it all.

Still exposed on IB

Microbatching is why the experts don't get the data-parallel treatment. For the non-experts, each microbatch gathers the weights for the forward, gathers them again for the backward, and reduce-scatters the gradients into the node's FP32 shards: three moves per microbatch, all on NVLink. IB only sees the once-per-step sync. The experts' weights stay resident, but their gradients can't be accumulated locally: an unsharded FP32 gradient for our slice is 38.1 GiB, which takes the total to 110 GiB. So the experts run ZeRO-2 over IB, with a BF16 reduce-scatter across the 32 replicas every microbatch and one gather of the updated weights at the end: nine moves, 3.6 s.

Against the expert-resident variant from 03, HSDP deletes 21 of 24 non-expert IB moves, 1.79 s, which leaves 9.2 s of IB against 6.8 s of compute. The non-expert traffic was never the big part: the hierarchy had already put seven-eighths of it on NVLink, and its IB share was an eighth of a 17-billion-parameter blob. What remains is traffic that scales with tokens or microbatches, not with parameters: EP's 5.4 s, and the experts' 3.6 s.

Could the experts get the same trick? Laying EP out across nodes, one GPU per node in each EP group, would give every node eight copies of one slice to shard among themselves. But then every routed copy crosses IB: about eight per token instead of 3.5, and a node limit of four would now mean four GPUs, 16 of the 256 experts.

And on an H800, NVLink too

Moving bytes onto NVLink assumes NVLink has room. HSDP doesn't add NVLink traffic. Each non-expert move puts seven-eighths of the BF16 non-expert weights on NVLink, whether it's a hierarchical FSDP gather or an HSDP one; HSDP only deletes the IB leg. But it makes NVLink the place where all the gather traffic lands. At 160 GB/s the 24 moves are 4.5 s, on top of the 3.2 s EP spends forwarding tokens from the GPU their IB copy lands on to their experts' GPUs:3 7.7 s in all, more than the 6.8 s of compute.

That's an H800 number. The H800 is an H100 with its NVLink cut from 900 GB/s to 400 (both directions combined), and the same tensor-core peaks. Scaling DeepSeek's 160 GB/s by the same ratio, an H100's NVLink would carry the same traffic in about 3.4 s, comfortably under compute. But the H100's IB is no faster, so HSDP still comes out at 9.2 s of IB there. A better NVLink leaves the experts as the only problem, but doesn't solve it. This is the same squeeze that makes tensor parallelism expensive in 03: both spend NVLink, and the H800 has little to spare.

Summary

  • For IB, the shard group is all or nothing: any group that crosses nodes pays nearly full-FSDP IB per gather, and HSDP over the node is data parallelism.
  • That layout technically fits, at 73.5 GiB, but only with the optimizer state sharded over all 2,048 GPUs and one sequence per microbatch.
  • It removes 1.79 s of IB and leaves 9.2 s, because EP and the experts' per-microbatch gradient reduce-scatters don't depend on how the non-experts are sharded.
  • On an H800 it also puts 7.7 s on NVLink, so the step would be bound on both links; an H100's NVLink would have room.

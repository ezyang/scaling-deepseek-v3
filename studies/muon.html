[[AI: Part 3’s roofline never prices the optimizer step. For AdamW that is defensible: the step is a handful of elementwise operations per parameter, once per step, bound by HBM bandwidth, and at DeepSeek’s batch of 62.9 M tokens it comes to 2.15 ms, 0.0324 % of the 6.64 s step. This appendix asks what changes in a hypothetical DeepSeek-V3 trained with Muon instead, keeping everything else in part 3 fixed: the H800 cluster, the PP 8 × EP 64 DualPipeV layout, the FP8 recipe, the batch.]]

[[AI: Muon orthogonalizes each weight matrix’s momentum with a few Newton–Schulz iterations, and those are matrix multiplies on the tensor cores, the same kind of work part 3’s T+c counts. So they belong on the roofline. The findings:]]

  1. [[AI: Newton–Schulz is 0.234 % of 6ND. 32.3 PFLOP per step, almost all of it in the routed experts, whose matrices see only D · k / E tokens a step. Done once per matrix, it takes 29.9 ms, set by the single largest matrix rather than the average: 0.482 % of the step, optimizer elementwise work included.]]
  2. [[AI: Who runs it matters more than how much there is. Moonlight’s Distributed Muon has every data-parallel rank orthogonalize every full matrix it holds a shard of. The non-expert matrices then run 2,048 / 8 = 256 times over, and Newton–Schulz takes 357 ms, 12.0 × as long: 5.41 % of the step. Its extra traffic hides under compute either way.]]
  3. [[AI: Small batches make it worse. Newton–Schulz runs once a step whatever the batch. At the start of DeepSeek’s batch ramp (3,072 sequences) the replicated scheme costs 27.9 % of compute.]]

Newton–Schulz is matmul work

[[AI: Each iteration on an m × n momentum matrix (m ≤ n) forms A = X·Xᵀ (2m²n FLOPs), B = bA + cA² (2m³) and B·X (2m²n). Five iterations cost 5 · (4m²n + 2m³) per matrix per step. Training costs that matrix 6mnT, where T is the number of tokens through it, so the ratio is about (10/3) · (m / T) · (1 + m / 2n). The optimizer step is spread over the batch, as for AdamW, but against tokens per matrix rather than tokens per step. A routed expert sees D · k / E = 1/32 of the batch, so its ratio, 0.397 %, is about twelve times that of the attention matrices. Making experts finer at a fixed parameter count (E up, de down) leaves m / T unchanged; only higher sparsity (E / k up at a fixed expert width) raises it.]]

Who runs Newton–Schulz

[[AI: ZeRO-1 shards the optimizer state as one flat buffer, so a matrix’s momentum is spread over its data-parallel group: the 256 GPUs of a pipeline stage for a non-expert matrix, the 2,048 / (64 · 8) = 4 replicas of an expert slice for a routed one. Newton–Schulz needs the whole matrix. Two published schemes put it together differently.]]

  • [[AI: Replicated (Moonlight’s Distributed Muon, Algorithm 1): every rank all-gathers the momentum of every matrix it holds a shard of, runs Newton–Schulz on the full matrix, and keeps its own slice of the result. Simple, but each non-expert matrix is orthogonalized 256 times and each expert matrix 4 times.]]
  • [[AI: Each matrix once (Kimi K3’s P2P scheme, K3 report §5.2.2): each matrix has an owner, which fetches the other shards, orthogonalizes, and sends the slices back. The FLOPs are 32.3 PFLOP spread over the cluster, except that a matrix can’t be split: the dense FFN matrices (22.6 TFLOP each) take longer than the per-GPU average, so they set the time.]]

What this does not model

  • [[AI: Overlap. The owners of early-finishing chunks could start Newton–Schulz inside the pipeline’s drain, and K3 pipelines its gathers with Newton–Schulz chunk by chunk. Both would hide part of the cost; this page charges all of it after the drain.]]
  • [[AI: Splitting a large matrix’s Newton–Schulz over several GPUs, which would bring the each-matrix-once time down to the per-GPU average; using the symmetry of X·Xᵀ and A² (a SYRK) to halve those products.]]
  • [[AI: Per-head orthogonalization (K3 §2.5), which splits the q and kv up-projections into per-head blocks and makes them cheaper; their Newton–Schulz is a small part of the total here.]]
  • [[AI: Load imbalance between pipeline stages (the embedding and head stay on AdamW) and between owners; the rate of small BF16 GEMMs at Newton–Schulz’s shapes, priced here at part 3’s measured large-GEMM rate.]]
  • [[AI: Muon’s training-efficiency claims (Moonlight reports matching AdamW at about 52 % of the FLOPs): this page prices a step, not a run.]]
  • [[AI: The MTP module, out of scope as in parts 2 and 3.]]

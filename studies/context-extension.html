The roofline of DeepSeek-V3’s context extension

DRAFT (unlisted study). Generated from ~/Dev/scaling-dsv3-refs/context-extension/roofline-gen.mjs — edit the generator, not this file.

DeepSeek-V3 pretrained on 14.8T tokens in 4,096-token sequences, then stretched its context window to 128K in two short phases: 1,000 steps at 32K tokens per sequence, then 1,000 at 128K, with YaRN rescaling the rotary frequencies of the decoupled rope key. The report gives the recipe in one paragraph (§4.3) and its cost in one cell of Table 1: 119K H800 GPU-hours, against 2,664K for pretraining. It says nothing about how the extension was laid out, and DeepSeek released no trace of it. Part 3’s roofline still gets two findings out of those numbers.

  1. The extension’s premium is its attention FLOPs. Speed of light predicts that an extension token costs 5.44 × as much as a pretraining token; DeepSeek’s numbers give 5.25 ×. This holds for any layout.
  2. At 128K, what context parallelism moves decides the cost. Under pretraining’s recompute policy a 128K sequence has to be split over at least 32 GPUs. Passing up-projected K and V between them would then cost 184 s to 221 s of InfiniBand per 128K step, 88.1 % to 106 % of the 209 s DeepSeek spent on a 32K and a 128K step together. Gathering MLA’s 576-wide latent instead, the design DeepSeek’s V4 report describes, costs 3.11 s. These floors charge no compute, so they don’t depend on kernel efficiency. Either the 128K phase was InfiniBand-bound for nearly all of its time, or it moved something much smaller than K and V, or it recomputed more than pretraining did.

The rest of the page is softer. DeepSeek’s pretraining trace says attention kernels run close to their roofline, which leaves part of the 119K unexplained, and a sweep over context-parallel layouts asks which layouts could account for it. Those results depend on two calibration ratios and are illustrative. YaRN itself costs nothing here: it changes the rotary frequencies and scales the attention logits, not the shapes of any matmul.

What DeepSeek published

One detail of the recipe does most of the work: the batch shrinks exactly as the sequence grows. 15,360 × 4K, 1,920 × 32K and 480 × 128K are all 62.9 M tokens per step. The two phases together are 126 G tokens, under 1 % of pretraining, for 4.5 % of its GPU-hours: a context-extension token cost rH = 5.25 × as much as a pretraining token.

Attention is the only term that grows

Finding 1: the premium is the attention FLOPs

Charge each phase its compute plus pretraining’s DualPipeV bubble, which holds for any layout that keeps 60 microbatches per replica and hides its communication. At speed of light the two phases take 41.1 K GPU-hours against the 119 K DeepSeek reports: speed of light is 2.90 × faster than reality, against 3.00 × for pretraining. Put another way, speed of light predicts that an extension token costs 5.44 × as much as a pretraining token, and DeepSeek measured 5.25 ×. To first order, the extra attention FLOPs account for the extension’s whole per-token premium. That compares two published totals; it says nothing about how the time split inside a step.

A long sequence does not fit on one GPU

Part 2 fit pretraining into 80 GiB with 4,096-token microbatches, eight and a half of them in flight under DualPipeV. Saved activations are linear in a microbatch’s tokens (the attention kernel saves its output and log-sum-exp, never the S · S scores), so under DeepSeek’s recompute policy the same budget holds at most 5.93 K tokens per GPU per microbatch. A 32K sequence then has to be spread over at least six GPUs, a 128K sequence over at least 23. The bound doesn’t hinge on part 2’s pipeline. What a GPU holds in flight is roughly one model’s worth of layer activations under any schedule DeepSeek might run: 8 layers × 8.5 microbatches here, about 4 layers × 17 under DualPipe on 16 ranks.

Finding 2: at 128K, what context parallelism moves decides the cost

Under DeepSeek’s recompute, then, a 128K sequence spans at least 23 GPUs, and in practice 32: a 217-token sequence splits evenly only over a power of two. A GPU holding one slice still needs the keys and values of every earlier slice. There are three standard ways to get them.

  • K/V passing. Ring attention passes up-projected K and V around the CP group; Llama 3 all-gathers them instead. Both move every other slice’s K and V to every GPU, 81.9 KB per token and layer. A causally aware variant sends each GPU only the chunks its queries can see. With zig-zag slicing, though, some GPU always holds the sequence’s last chunk and still needs everything.
  • Latent all-gather. MLA offers something much smaller to gather: the 576-wide latent that inference caches, 1.15 KB. The price is that every GPU re-runs the kv up-projection for every token it gathers. This is the shape of DeepSeek’s own context parallelism two generations later: the V4 report (§3.4.3) all-gathers its compressed KV entries across CP ranks.
  • Head-parallel (DeepSpeed-Ulysses). It all-to-alls Q, K and V from a sequence split into a head split, and O back, so each GPU’s bytes don’t grow with CP.

At CP 32, 24 of a GPU’s 31 CP peers are on other nodes. Passing every slice puts 11.1 TB per GPU per step through its InfiniBand NIC: forward once, then backward again plus the dK and dV partials. Holding a 128K sequence’s gathered K and V for every layer in flight instead would take tens of GB. That is 221 s per 128K step even with compute free and every other transfer overlapped, 106 % of the reported time for a 32K and a 128K step together. Causal sends trim the busiest GPU to 184 s, still 88.1 % of it. At the same CP the latent all-gather needs 3.11 s and head-parallel attention 9.21 s.

So if DeepSeek passed K and V under its pretraining recompute policy, the 128K phase spent nearly the whole reported budget waiting on InfiniBand, and phase 1 had to fit in what was left. That isn’t impossible, but it is tight. There are two easier ways out. One is to change the policy: recomputing every layer from its input fits 65K tokens per GPU, so a 128K sequence needs only two GPUs and K and V can stay on NVLink (priced below). The other is to shrink the bytes: MLA’s latent is 71 times smaller than K and V. DeepSeek’s V4 report (§3.4.3) describes exactly that design for its successor, an all-gather of compressed KV entries across CP ranks. Offloading activations to host memory is the one route this page doesn’t price.

How fast attention actually runs

DeepSeek’s released H800 profile is a pretraining run (EP64, 4,096-token microbatches), but it does include the FlashAttention kernels for MLA’s 192-wide query-key heads. The forward and backward together run 1.54 × slower than their speed-of-light time, far closer to their roofline than the step as a whole. Charge the rest of pretraining’s gap to everything else: 3.45 ×. That ratio absorbs whatever pretraining spent beyond compute, exposed communication included, and assumes the extension spent it the same way.

Price the extension at those two ratios and it should have cost 74.2 K GPU-hours, not 119 K. That leaves 78.8 s per pair of steps (one at 32K, one at 128K) that attention FLOPs don’t explain. If attention kernels got more efficient at long sequences, as they usually do, the gap only widens. Something that grows with sequence length cost the extension more than a third of its time. The rest of the page asks whether context parallelism, the thing the extension had to add, can be that something. From here on, unlike the two findings, everything rests on these two ratios.

Recompute, priced

Finding 2 assumed DeepSeek kept their pretraining recompute policy. Recomputing every layer from its input (set frc = 1) keeps only 14 KB per token per layer: 65K tokens fit, so a 32K sequence fits on one GPU and a 128K sequence needs two. Full recompute isn’t free, though. It replays the whole forward: the GEMMs, and an extra attention forward that grows the attention count by 28 %. Both are priced at the measured ratios, and the sweep below runs both policies.

The layout: context parallelism

DeepSeek trained without tensor or context parallelism, and the pretraining layout can’t run the extension unchanged. At PP 16 each replica takes 491,520 tokens per step, which is 15 sequences at 32K (DualPipe needs an even number of microbatches) and 3.75 at 128K. So something changed. We assume context parallelism: CP GPUs share a sequence, a replica is PP · CP GPUs, and a microbatch is one sequence. CP1 and CP2 are knobs, set to the smallest powers of two that fit under DeepSeek’s recompute. There every GPU keeps pretraining’s 4,096-token slice and 60 microbatches.

Which layouts fit the 119K

The reported cost is 209 s per pair of steps. The sheet prices one layout at a time. The tables below run every power-of-two CP from 1 to 256 in each phase through the same formulas, under both recompute policies, assuming the same scheme and policy in both phases. A combination counts only if each phase’s slice fits in memory and DualPipe gets an even number of microbatches. Unlike the two findings, every number here rests on κattn and κrest.

At the default rates, three of the eight scheme-and-recompute combinations land within 5 % of the 119K, and one more misses by about 12 %.

  • Ulysses never costs enough: 130 s per pair at most with DeepSeek’s recompute, 173 s with full recompute. Its traffic always hides and it adds no FLOPs, so it can’t produce the leftover.
  • K/V passing with DeepSeek’s recompute costs too much, but not by much once sends are causal: 272 s at best for every slice, 234 s with causal sends, 12 % over. That is finding 2 again, now with compute charged. It sits outside the ±5 % band but well within the calibration’s uncertainty.
  • K/V passing with full recompute fits at CP 16/16 when every slice is sent. Full recompute lets a 128K slice grow to 8K tokens, so CP 16 suffices, and InfiniBand is then exposed but not overwhelming. With causal sends it gets cheaper and falls short of the band (193 s at closest).
  • The latent all-gather fits a band under both policies: CP 8/128, 16/128, 64/64 with DeepSeek’s recompute, CP 4/64, 8/64, 16/64, 32/32, 64/16 with full recompute. Its traffic hides, and the replayed up-projections, which grow with CP, supply the leftover.

So the 119K is consistent with DeepSeek’s later design, the latent all-gather, at a CP well above the minimum the memory needs. It is also consistent with K/V passing under full recompute, and nearly with K/V passing under DeepSeek’s own recompute. The total alone can’t tell them apart; a trace of either phase could. The reverse engineering is only as good as its two ratios. κattn comes from 4K kernels, κrest lumps all of pretraining’s overheads into one number, and communication is charged at spec bandwidth with perfect overlap. A κattn of about 2.9 instead of 1.5, attention at long sequences running as far off its roofline as everything else, would close the gap with no help from context parallelism.

What this does not model

  • DeepSeek’s own pipeline. Their run used DualPipe on 16 ranks; this page keeps parts 2 and 3’s 8-rank DualPipeV, whose memory part 2 measured. At PP 16 the even-microbatch rule allows different CP degrees.
  • Non-power-of-two CP degrees, tensor parallelism, activation offload, and a different cluster size or pipeline depth for the extension.
  • The MTP module, out of scope as in parts 2 and 3. Its one extra attention layer would add 1.6 % to the attention count.
  • Contention: CP shares NVLink with EP’s intra-node forwarding and InfiniBand with EP itself. They are priced on the same link as if they took turns, never as competing for the SMs DeepSeek reserves for communication.
  • Causal load imbalance across a CP group (zig-zag slicing is assumed to balance it), and the recompute transient (one layer’s full activations during replay).
  • Whatever the 119K GPU-hours include beyond the 2,000 training steps: evaluation, checkpointing, restarts.

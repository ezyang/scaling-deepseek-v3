The roofline of DeepSeek-V3’s context extension

DRAFT (unlisted study). Generated from ~/Dev/scaling-dsv3-refs/context-extension/roofline-gen.mjs — edit the generator, not this file.

DeepSeek-V3 pretrained on 14.8T tokens in 4,096-token sequences, then stretched its context window to 128K in two short phases: 1,000 steps at 32K tokens per sequence, then 1,000 at 128K, with YaRN rescaling the rotary frequencies of the decoupled rope key. The report gives the whole recipe in one paragraph (§4.3) and its cost in one cell of Table 1: 119K H800 GPU-hours, against 2,664K for pretraining. This page runs part 3’s roofline on those two phases and asks how far from speed of light they ran. YaRN itself costs nothing here: it changes the rotary frequencies and scales the attention logits, not the shapes of any matmul.

What DeepSeek published

One detail of the recipe does most of the work: the batch shrinks exactly as the sequence grows. 15,360 × 4K, 1,920 × 32K and 480 × 128K are all 62.9 M tokens per step. The two phases together are 126 G tokens, under 1 % of pretraining, for 4.5 % of its GPU-hours: a context-extension token cost rH = 5.25 × as much as a pretraining token.

Attention is the only term that grows

Part 3’s attention count, L · B · (4 · cqk + 3 · cpv), is α · D · S once the per-sequence S · S is written out. At fixed D it grows linearly with S: 1.49 s per step at 4K, 11.9 s at 32K, 47.8 s at 128K. That takes the attention core from a quarter of the step’s compute to more than nine tenths of it. This counts every causal pair of the packed sequence: the report says pretraining packs documents without cross-sample attention masking, and we assume the extension does too.

A long sequence does not fit on one GPU

Part 2 fit pretraining into 80 GiB with 4,096-token microbatches, eight and a half of them in flight under DualPipeV. Saved activations are linear in a microbatch’s tokens (the attention kernel saves its output and log-sum-exp, never the S · S scores), so the same budget holds at most 5.93 K tokens per GPU per microbatch. A 32K sequence has to be spread over at least six GPUs, a 128K sequence over at least 23. DeepSeek trained without tensor or context parallelism, and the report doesn’t say how the extension was laid out. The pretraining layout can’t run it unchanged in any case: at PP 16 each replica takes 491,520 tokens per step, which is 15 sequences at 32K (DualPipe needs an even number of microbatches) and 3.75 at 128K.

We pick the layout that leaves each GPU’s work exactly as it was: context parallelism CP = S / 4,096, so every GPU still holds a 4,096-token slice of one sequence per microbatch. Its activation memory is part 2’s, and so is everything that is counted per token.

What context parallelism moves

A GPU holding one slice of a sequence still needs the keys and values of every earlier slice. There are three standard ways to get them. Ring attention, which is what Megatron’s context parallelism does, passes K and V around the CP group. MLA offers something much smaller to pass: the 576-wide latent that inference caches. The price is that every GPU then re-runs the kv up-projection for every token it gathers. Head-parallel attention (DeepSpeed-Ulysses) all-to-alls Q, K and V from a sequence split into a head split and O back, so each GPU’s bytes don’t grow with CP. Up-projected K and V are 81.9 KB per token and layer, 71 times the latent’s 1.15 KB.

Phase 2: CP 32 across four nodes

At CP 32, 77.4 % of a GPU’s CP peers are on other nodes, and those bytes cross InfiniBand. Ring attention would need 221 s of InfiniBand time per step, about four times the compute, so ring CP at this size is not an option on an H800’s 50 GB/s. The latent all-gather needs 3.11 s of InfiniBand plus 5.35 s of replayed FP8 compute. Ulysses needs 9.21 s. Added to the unchanged traffic, InfiniBand is busy for 28.6 % of phase 2’s compute time, so it hides under compute. The step times below assume Ulysses (or anything else whose traffic hides and that adds no FLOPs). The latent all-gather would add its 5.35 s of replay to phase 2’s compute instead.

Putting it together

At speed of light the two phases take 41.1 K GPU-hours against the 119 K DeepSeek reports: speed of light is 2.90 × faster than reality, against 3.00 × for pretraining. Put another way, speed of light predicts that an extension token costs 5.44 × as much as a pretraining token, and DeepSeek measured 5.25 ×. The extra attention FLOPs account for the whole of the extension’s per-token premium. The run sat at about the same distance from speed of light as pretraining did.

Where the extension’s time went

Equal ratios hide a puzzle. Attention at long sequence lengths is the friendliest kernel in the step: large, dense, compute-bound, with no all-to-all in its way. If it had run near its roofline while everything else stayed three times off, the extension would have been much cheaper than reported. Splitting the reported cost into two ratios, one for attention and one for the rest, each held fixed across the three phases, pins both: attention ran 2.87 × below its roofline, everything else 3.04 × below its own. Attention ran as far from speed of light as the MoE machinery did.

The roofline can’t say why, but it can list what κattn absorbs. Anything that grows with S counts against attention here: CP traffic that wasn’t hidden (ring attention doesn’t fit under compute even inside a node), causal load imbalance across a CP group without zig-zag slicing, and attention kernels for MLA’s 192-wide query-key head that in late 2024 had nothing like FlashAttention-3’s tuning. It works the other way too: if the extension’s data masked documents from each other, there were fewer attention FLOPs than counted here, and attention ran even further off.

What this does not model

  • The layout. DeepSeek doesn’t say how the extension was parallelized or on how many GPUs. Under a compute-bound roofline GPU-hours don’t depend on the cluster size (GPUs cancels out of every ratio above), but the bubble and the CP traffic do depend on the layout.
  • The MTP module, out of scope as in parts 2 and 3. Its one extra attention layer would add 1.6 % to the attention count.
  • Contention: in phase 1, CP and EP’s intra-node forwarding share NVLink; in phase 2, CP shares InfiniBand with EP, and the two are summed here as if one link carried them in turn.
  • Whatever the 119K GPU-hours include beyond the 2,000 training steps: evaluation, checkpointing, restarts.

Does it fit?

[[AI: Yes, with room to spare. Everything is sharded over all 256 GPUs, so parameters, gradients and optimizer state together come to under a gibibyte per GPU. What remains is what is in flight: two gathered layers (the one at work and the one prefetched), the head, which FlexShard keeps gathered until backward ends, and the activations. SelectiveAC saves the outputs of compute-heavy ops and replays the rest. In this model that saves the block input, Q, the up-projected K and V, the attention output and the shared experts’ gate/up output, and saves nothing from the routed experts: their grouped GEMM is not on its save list and the eager all-to-all cannot be saved at all. A MoE layer keeps 33.9 KB per token. The peak sits at the start of backward, when every layer’s stash is still held and the last layer replays its experts and builds their gradients.]]

[[AI: The tally is 22.3 GiB, 28.2 % of an H100, against the 24.8 GiB peak PyTorch reserved on rank 0. Rank 0 should sit above an even split: FlexShard assigns whole matrices to ranks, largest first, so the low ranks own each layer’s biggest matrices, and the allocator adds fragmentation on top of the live bytes. At 32 GPUs the state per GPU grows eightfold, and the tally follows the measured peak there too (the table below). Recomputing less would be the natural use of the spare memory: all of the replay is in the routed experts.]]

Comparing against TorchTitan’s numbers

[[AI: TorchTitan logs 7.7 % MFU for this run. Its numerator counts 6N plus attention as full and non-causal, with no backward replay and no recompute, over the 989 TFLOP/s spec peak. On that scale the speed of light here would be 69.1 %.]]

[[AI: Two measurements say the gap is not the roofline’s communication. First, the 32-GPU runs: the same model, the same 16,384 tokens per GPU, EP 8, dp_shard 32. Per GPU the roofline barely changes, since a ring moves (n − 1)/n of a shard set whatever n is: the expert collectives go from 3/4 to 31/32 of the slice. Measured, the step goes from 2.29 s to 3.91 s. Second, the FP8 all-gather: 4.33 K tokens/s per GPU, +3.2 %. In the source tree that recipe converts only the attention projections and the shared and dense FFNs to blockwise FP8 (the run’s own recipe is not in source control), so the routed experts, which carry most of the gathered bytes, still move in BF16. The roofline, compute bound with scale-out at 70.1 %, predicts almost nothing from either change. Both moved the measured step, so something scale-dependent is exposed that bandwidth does not explain: per-collective latency across 32 nodes, waits on the slowest rank, the uneven ownership of whole matrices.]]

[[AI: Suspects the source shows and the trace can settle (the job kept profiler traces at step 100): a blocking device-to-host sync in every MoE layer’s dispatch (split sizes, before the all-to-all), a blocking all-reduce for the expert-bias update every step, prefetch one layer deep, no CUDA graphs, eager everywhere except attention, SwiGLU and the loss, and, if FlexShard’s end-of-backward hook fires per loss chunk as the source suggests, eight gathers and reduce-scatters of the head per step instead of one.]]

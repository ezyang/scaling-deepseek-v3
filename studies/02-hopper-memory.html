A DSv3-family MoE on 2048 H100s: memory

Does it even fit on 80 GB — and what the aggressive recompute policy buys and costs.

Configuration. A 288-routed-expert DSv3-family variant (62 MoE layers — modeled all-sparse, a simplification; the real family stacks 3 dense blocks first, see the structure widget below — vocab 128000, no MTP) trained on 2048 H100-class GPUs: FSDP-replicas 4 × PP 8 × EP 64 (our decomposition: gpus=2048, pp=8, ep=64 → dp=256, dp/ep=4), seq 4096, mbs 1, 64 microbatches. Precision: FP8 GEMMs with 1×128 tile scales (weights kept ×2 for the transposed copy, +4/128 fp32 scale bytes), fp32 gradient accumulators, a 3-state AMSGrad optimizer sharded over all 2048 GPUs. Recompute: the attn-replay policy — replay all of attention (norm, down/up projections, SDPA core, out-proj, and the residual add) from the block input during backward, save the post-norm output plus the fp8 expert inputs and gate/up, recompute swiglu-out. Pipeline in-flight: DualPipe's two-chunk layout holds 17 microbatches × 4 layers per chunk ≡ 8.5 microbatches/rank.

Our per-rank inventory

Why this fits on 80 GB. Three modeling points carry the number. (1) Gating placement: the router's gate weights are applied to the swiglu output before the linear down-proj — mathematically identical to gating the expert outputs, and it makes the expert outputs a pure intermediate rather than a ~30 GiB stash for the combine's backward. (2) The attn-replay policy: because all of attention replays from the block input, the mid-layer residual (x1), the MLA latents, the softmax lse, and the SDPA output are never stashed — the attention side of the block saves exactly one tensor, the post-norm output that the MoE half consumes. The cost is ~+40% of forward FLOPs replayed in backward: the Hopper-era activation-checkpointing pressure in one number. (3) The in-flight law: DualPipe's two-chunk schedule halves the naive (2·PP+1) in-flight count per layer, so the activation column is 8.5×, not 17×.

Layer structure

Structure disclosure: everything above models all 62 layers as MoE. The real model family stacks 3 dense-FFN blocks (inter 18432) before the sparse ones to stabilize early routing. The pinned row below stays on the simplification; the toggle patches the exploration row with the structure you pick (dense first layers trade expert stashes for a fatter FFN on rank 0).

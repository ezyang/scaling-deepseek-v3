Pricing tensor and context parallelism for DeepSeek-V3

[[AI: DeepSeek uses neither, but it's worth pricing them anyway, because the roofline says why. Both put more than one GPU on a single sequence: tensor parallelism (TP) splits each layer's weights, context parallelism (CP) splits the sequence. That buys two things: less memory per GPU, and a cluster that can keep growing after the pipeline replicas run out of microbatches.

DSv3 at 2,048 GPUs needs neither. The memory post already fit, with about 18 GiB to spare. As for the batch: without TP or CP, the smallest unit of work is one 4,096-token sequence on one pipeline, and each replica runs 120 of them per step, for a 3.9% DualPipe bubble. The cluster could quadruple to 8,192 GPUs before that got uncomfortable (30 microbatches per replica, a bubble of 14F out of 90F = 15.6%). So the questions are what TP and CP would cost, and whether the memory they free could buy anything back.]]

Tensor parallelism

[[AI: In Megatron's layout for DSv3, TP splits the attention up-projections and output projection, the shared expert and the dense FFNs across the GPUs of a node; the routed experts stay under EP. With sequence parallelism, each of those blocks is bracketed by an all-gather of the residual stream going in and a reduce-scatter coming out: four collectives per layer forward, and four more backward. (Megatron also re-gathers inputs in the backward pass for the weight gradients, and the output head adds a pair of its own; we'll leave those out.) In each collective over t GPUs, a GPU receives the other t − 1 GPUs' share of the BF16 residual stream, so per token it owns, per layer, it receives:

8 × (t − 1) × 14,336 B

This stays inside the node, on NVLink rather than IB. But NVLink isn't idle: EP's forwarding, from the GPU a token's IB copy lands on to the GPUs holding its experts, keeps it busy about 60% as long as IB, 3.2 s per step. At 160 GB/s, over 61 layers and 30,720 tokens per GPU:

Against 8.0 s of compute at the BF16 peak (6.9 s effective, per 03's effective roofline) and 5.8 s of IB, TP8 makes NVLink the new bottleneck, stretching the step by about 60%, and TP4 leaves no room: its 7.2 s is more than the effective compute. The H800's cut-down NVLink (400 GB/s, against the H100's 900) is a big part of this, and it's the cost DeepSeek means when its report says its memory savings let it train "without using costly Tensor Parallelism (TP)." TP2 fits comfortably, even under IB.

What would TP2 buy? At the memory post's configuration it takes the fullest GPU from 61.7 GiB to 36.5 GiB, mostly activations, since each GPU now holds half of every microbatch's tokens. That's enough to turn recompute off entirely (58.2 GiB), which saves 0.21 s of compute (the same at effective rates). So we'd pay 1.3 s of NVLink to save 0.2 s of compute. And the NVLink time isn't free even when there's slack: each all-gather has to land before the GEMM that consumes it, so hiding it takes overlap kernels with SMs of their own, and in the effective roofline SMs are the binding resource. The GEMMs get worse too: the shared expert's 2,048-wide intermediate becomes 1,024 at TP2 and 256 at TP8.

Asymptotically, TP moves 16·(t − 1)·h bytes per token per layer while the matmuls grow like h², so like PP, TP gets relatively cheaper as models get wider. But it gets more expensive as t grows: each GPU does 1/t of a token's compute, yet still receives (t − 1)/t of its activations, so communication per unit of compute grows like t − 1. That's why TP stays small and inside the fastest interconnect available.]]

Context parallelism

[[AI: CP splits each sequence across c GPUs. Everything except attention works token by token, so it runs unchanged; attention needs the keys and values of every earlier token. MLA can make this very cheap. A token's keys and values are up-projected from a 576-element latent (512 compressed plus 64 RoPE), 1,152 B in BF16. All-gathering the latents forward and reduce-scattering their gradients backward is 2 × (c − 1) × 1,152 B per token per layer: at CP2, 4.3 GB per GPU per step, 0.09 s even over IB. The catch is that every GPU then up-projects the whole sequence's latents rather than just its own, about 0.2 s of extra compute at CP2.

A generic ring-attention implementation only sees attention's inputs, so it passes the up-projected K and V instead: 128 heads × (192 + 128) × 2 B = 81,920 B per token, 71× the latent. Forward and backward, that's at least 2 × (c − 1) × 81,920 B per token per layer: 6.1 s on IB at CP2, or 1.9 s if the CP group stays inside a node. Whether CP is cheap depends on which tensor you send.

Either way, what CP buys is memory, and only activation memory: a CP group holds full copies of the weights, just like DP. CP2 halves activations just as TP2 does, which again is enough to turn recompute off (63.0 GiB), and again that's worth 0.2 s. CP earns its keep on long sequences, where one sequence's activations stop fitting on one GPU. Its asymptotics point the same way: the bytes per token are fixed by the KV width, but attention compute per token grows with sequence length, so the longer the sequence, the cheaper CP gets relative to the work it splits. At 4,096 tokens there's nothing for it to do.]]

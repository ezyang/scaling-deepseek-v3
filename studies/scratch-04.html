How EP scales

[[AI: Let's apply asymptotics to EP. Per token, per MoE layer, the routed experts do 3 matmuls (gate, up, down) of h × I each, for each of k experts, at 6 FLOPs per parameter forward and backward: 18·h·I·k FLOPs. EP moves h elements to n remote nodes, twice (dispatch and combine) in each of two passes, at 1 byte (FP8) plus 2 bytes (BF16): 6·h·n bytes. With peak FLOP rate F and IB bandwidth B, the ratio of communication time to routed-expert compute time is:

(6·h·n / B) ÷ (18·h·I·k / F) = n·F / (3·I·k·B)

The hidden size cancels. Making the model wider does nothing for EP unless the experts get wider too. (In the scaling family from earlier, they do: I grows with h, so EP gets relatively cheaper as models grow, just like PP.) What matters is expert width I, the number of experts per token k, how many nodes a token touches n, and the hardware's ratio F/B, which is 19,780 BF16 FLOPs per IB byte on H800.

EP time ÷ compute time (6ND at BF16 peak) per step on H800s, across the scaling ladder: EP64 over 8 nodes, 3.5 remote nodes per token, FP8 dispatch with its scales, BF16 combine. At the small end, the fixed-vocabulary lm head pads compute.

For DSv3 (n = 3.5, I = 2,048, k = 8), EP takes 1.4× as long as the routed-expert matmuls it feeds: 5.4 s against 3.8 s. To break even, experts would need I ≈ 2,900, 40% wider. This is the tension in fine-grained MoE: splitting a fixed budget of active parameters into more, narrower experts keeps I·k constant, so compute doesn't change, but each token fans out to more nodes. Node-limited routing is DeepSeek's answer: it caps n at 4 no matter how many experts a token picks. And F/B is the reason GB200 changes the picture: inside an NVL72 domain, B is NVLink's 900 GB/s rather than IB's 50 GB/s.]]

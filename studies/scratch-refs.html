The peak column is a unit, not a prediction: it's how long the work would take if every tensor core ran flat out, which no real training run comes close to. The sustained column is measured throughput, with downtime left out. On the H800s it is DeepSeek's own: the report prices pretraining at 180K H800-hours per trillion tokens (2.664M for all 14.8T), and 6ND over that is 339 TFLOP/s per GPU, 34% of the BF16 peak. This is the MFU convention: anything that isn't one of the 6ND FLOPs (the attention core, pipeline bubbles, communication that wasn't hidden) counts as lost time. DeepSeek's own MFU counts the attention core as useful work too: it reports 385 TFLOP/s, 38.9% of the BF16 peak, and our 339 becomes 386 once the causal attention core is added. Within a group the two columns differ by the same ratio on every row: ×2.9 for DeepSeek.

OpenAI doesn't publish GPT-4's training compute, so that row is Epoch AI's estimate,Epoch AI, AI Models database, entry "GPT-4 (Mar 2023)": 2.1 × 1025 FLOP, 90% interval 8.2 × 1024 to 4.4 × 1025, marked a rough estimate built from much less public information than most entries. GPT-3's 3.14 × 1023, by contrast, is published (Brown et al. 2020, Table D.1). uncertain by 2–3× either way. The last group is a reminder that 2,048 H800s is no longer a big cluster. Its rate comes from MLPerf Training 6.0, whose DeepSeek-V3 benchmark is DeepSeek-V3 itself (MTP included, MXFP8) continuing from the released checkpoint at a batch of 16,384 sequences until the validation loss reaches a target, about 45 steps. CoreWeave's record run on 8,192 GB300s took a median 1.80 s per step, about 1,000 TFLOP/s per GPU in 6ND terms, 40% of the BF16 peak. The pretraining row imputes that rate over all 14.8T tokens, on the same footing as DeepSeek's own figure: both are throughput, and neither counts failures, checkpoint stalls, or the early batch-size ramp. How much a real run loses on top of that is rarely published; Microsoft's MAI-Base-1 (a 35B-active MoE, 30T tokens on 8,192 GB200s) is an exception. It sustained about 465 TFLOP/s per GPU in 6ND terms (Microsoft reports about 20% MFU against the BF16 peak, counting attention at its 16K context), and lost another 10% of wall time to failures and restarts, 21 days in all.

So one step is seven seconds of the whole cluster at peak and twenty sustained, and a whole pretraining run is about 235,000 of them (fewer in practice, since DeepSeek ramps the batch size up early on). At DeepSeek's own rental price of $2 per GPU-hour, a step costs about $23 at the sustained rate, or $8 at peak.

We'll soon be pricing bytes as well as FLOPs, so it helps to have the exchange rate. In one microsecond, one H800 can do 9.9 × 108 BF16 FLOPs, or move:

bytes in 1 µsBF16 FLOPs per byte
HBM (3.35 TB/s)3,350KB295
NVLink (160 GB/s)160KB6,180
InfiniBand (50 GB/s)50KB19,800

A byte sent over Infiniband costs as much time as twenty thousand FLOPs. A token needs 2.2 × 1011 FLOPs to train, so it can afford about 11.1 MB of IB traffic (10.6 MiB) before the network, not the tensor cores, sets the step time.]]

AMD and Google have published no DeepSeek-V3 training numbers.

From H800 to GB300, peak grows 2.5× but sustained grows 4.1×, and the table's pairs show the peak isn't what moved it. GB200 and GB300 have identical peaks, yet GB300 sustains 28% more (NVIDIA's recipe for it uses two pipeline stages instead of four, presumably room its 1.5× memory makes). B300 is the same chip as GB300 but sustains 56% as much: its NVLink domain is 8 GPUs, not 72, so expert parallelism has to cross the slower network. Precision moves it less than you'd think: in one earlier NVIDIA release, switching the same GB300 recipe from BF16 to MXFP8 doubled every GEMM's peak and gained 13%. And software moves it more: the GB300 figure rose 45% between two releases seven months apart. HBM bandwidth has grown in step with peak (3.35 → 8 TB/s), so the FLOPs-per-byte exchange rate is about 300 on all of them.]]
